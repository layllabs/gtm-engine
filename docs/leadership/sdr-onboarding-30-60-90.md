# SDR onboarding, 30/60/90

The structure I use to onboard a new SDR. Built when I was leading a team
of five-plus across SMB and mid-market, revised each time someone went
through it. Specific numbers (quota ramp, activity targets) belong to the
team that uses it and are left as placeholders here.

Design principle: a new rep should be on live conversations in week one
with a script and a safety net, not studying for a month. Confidence comes
from reps, not slides.

## Before day one

- Tools provisioned and tested by someone else: CRM, sequencer, dialer,
  call recording, data provider, calendar. First day spent on access
  tickets is a lost day.
- A starter book: 50 accounts in the ICP, already enriched, with one signal
  each where possible. The rep's first job is to work it, not build it.
- A buddy assigned: a tenured rep, not the manager, for the questions
  people do not ask managers.

## Days 1 to 30: learn the motion by doing it

**Week 1**
- Day 1: who we sell to and why they buy, told through three customer
  stories, not a deck. Then listen to five recorded calls: two good, two
  average, one bad, with the rep guessing which is which before I say.
- Day 2: the sequence, line by line, with why each line is there. The rep
  rewrites one email in their own words; we compare.
- Day 3: role-play the opener and the first objection. Five rounds. Record
  it. Rep listens back alone.
- Day 4 and 5: live calls, buddy on the line, from the starter book. Goal:
  ten conversations, zero meetings required.

**Weeks 2 to 4**
- Daily: activity floor, not a ceiling. Calls, personalized emails,
  LinkedIn touches. Numbers set per team.
- Twice weekly: 20 minute call review from recordings. One thing to keep,
  one thing to change. Never more than one change at a time.
- Weekly: pipeline review with me. Every account in the book has a next
  step or a reason it does not.
- End of week 4: the rep writes a one-page "what I think our buyer
  actually cares about." Shows me whether they are listening on calls.

**Exit criteria for 30:** can run a cold call unassisted through the first
objection; sends personalized email without a template open; CRM hygiene
clean for two straight weeks; at least one meeting booked.

## Days 31 to 60: own the book

- Full account ownership. The starter book is theirs to grow and prune.
- Learn list building: ICP filters, signals, dedupe against existing
  customers. Build their next 50 accounts themselves, I review before
  they work it.
- Objection library: the rep adds one real objection a week with the
  response that worked and the one that did not.
- Shadow an AE on two discovery calls and one demo. Write up what the AE
  needed from the handoff that was missing.
- Call review moves to weekly, 30 minutes, rep picks the call.

**Exit criteria for 60:** meetings booked at the team's ramp target; at
least one meeting that converted to opportunity; can explain why each
account in the book is there.

## Days 61 to 90: consistency and judgment

- Full quota ramp. Activity floor still applies but conversion is what we
  talk about.
- The rep runs one team call: a win, a loss, what they learned. Everyone
  presents eventually; it is how the team learns and how I see who leads.
- A/B test of their own: the rep proposes one change to the sequence, we
  split the book, we read the result together with the z-test, not
  anecdotes.
- Multi-threading: on every opportunity they sourced, a second contact.
- 90 day review: against the written exit criteria, not vibes. What they
  own next: a vertical, a signal source, a piece of the sequence.

**Exit criteria for 90:** at quota ramp for two consecutive weeks; a
sequence change they proposed has data behind it; one piece of the team's
process they now own.

## Coaching rules I hold myself to

1. Coach from recordings, not memory. Gong or equivalent. Timestamps.
2. One change per review. People cannot fix five things at once.
3. Praise specifics. "Good call" teaches nothing. "You paused after the
   objection instead of filling the silence" does.
4. The rep picks half the calls we review. Otherwise I only see what I
   went looking for.
5. Role-play is not optional and I go first. Nobody wants to be bad at
   something in front of their manager; the manager being bad at it first
   fixes that.
6. Activity is a floor, not a goal. If someone hits the floor and books
   nothing for three weeks, the problem is not effort.

## What I would add next

A written "what good looks like" rubric per call stage, scored by the rep
and me independently, with the gap being the coaching agenda. I have done
this informally; it should be a document.
